## Details

### V1: the 2025 requests and commitments
Primary publication: SMARD (Bundesnetzagentur) topic article "Entwicklung bei ÜNB und VNB" / "Developments at TSOs and DSOs", dated **20.08.2026**, data year 2025 with 2024 as comparison and Stichtag 01.04.2026 for the 2026 ÜNB figures [1], [2]. Data come from the BNetzA monitoring survey of transmission (ÜNB) and distribution (VNB) system operators, "the second annual collection of this kind" [1]. Earlier first release: BNetzA press release of 12.11.2025 covering 2024 [3].

Exact figures (copied from [1]/[2]): requests 18,158 (573.5 GW; about 1,583 GWh); commitments 1,937 (54.2 GW; 142.4 GWh), which "include applications from prior years", whereas the breakdown "2025 applications" excludes them; commitments for 2025 applications only 1,468 (33.9 GW; 98.8 GWh). Percentages: VNB commitments for 2025 requests are about 8 % of requests (about 4 % of requested power); ÜNB about 10 % [1].

Definitions (SMARD): "Netzanschlussbegehren" = request to connect at a potential location, "Several applications may cover the same project", so counts overstate distinct projects; "Netzanschlusszusage" = "a binding offer to conclude a connection agreement", treated as a more realistic indicator [1], [2]. The 2024 press release adds that approvals "bind only the network operators, so not every approved project will necessarily be built by the applicant" [3]. Coverage: "commercial grid-scale battery storage at medium-voltage level and above"; home storage excluded; grid-coupled and co-located (plant-coupled) storage are not yet separated [1]. BNetzA AgNes slides (27.05.2026) reportedly say at least 50 % of requests will not be realised because of multiple filings or speculation (search summary, SECONDARY, slide deck not opened). Related earlier BNetzA figure: end of 2024 about 650 requests with 226 GW at the TSOs [4]. Trade press quoted "ca. 211 GW" TSO requests at end Q3 2025 for large storage [9] (SECONDARY).

Wording note: KICKOFF says "signed connection commitments". SMARD says "Zusage" = binding offer to conclude a contract ("verbindliches Angebot"), not a signed contract. Also "roughly 54 GW and 142 GWh" is the all-requests-year total (older requests included); the commitments that answer 2025 requests alone are 33.9 GW and 98.8 GWh. Since one project may hold commitments at several sites ("Only one site is usually realized when several are committed", pv magazine reading of the article [11], SECONDARY), the commitments total is an upper bound for build-out, not a forecast.

The number of large (> 1 MW) units added in 2025 differs between two trade-press readings of Battery-Charts (83 registrations / 667 MW / 1,244 MWh in [8], versus 61 / 520 MW / 767 MWh in another article seen only in a search summary). Cause unknown (snapshot date, gross vs net, size definition); treat the counts as approximate and recompute from our own MaStR download. Battery-Charts home page [12] displays no numbers in the fetched text, gives no licence and no definition; its data source is the public MaStR.

Rule for us: reproduce all of these from the MaStR export ourselves (filter `Bruttoleistung` > 1000 kW, status In Betrieb, sum `NutzbareSpeicherkapazitaet` from `AnlagenStromSpeicher`) and report the result next to the BNetzA figure with the size definition, Stichtag and gross vs net stated.

### Ground-mounted PV reference totals
- BNetzA press release 08.01.2026 [5]: 16.4 GW PV added in 2025; installed PV 117 GW at year end; about half buildings, half ground-mounted; method: "Zubau ... aus den aktuell in Betrieb befindlichen Anlagen abzüglich stillgelegter Anlagen"; basis MaStR registrations; December partly estimated. Gives no MW split for ground-mounted.
- BNetzA publishes monthly "Zubau" statistics (usually around the 15th, [13]); the monthly tables were not opened. Trade press reports, preliminary [6], [7]: March 2026 ground-mounted 154 new plants, 743.9 MW; April 526.7 MW; June 573 MW; August 977.2 MW from 149 parks (data as of 14.09.2026, BNetzA adds a 15 % buffer for the four-week registration window); Jan-Aug 6.27 GW ground-mounted vs 4.69 GW buildings; 2025 ground-mounted almost 8.2 GW; total installed PV just under 129.3 GW. Monthly values are revised upward with late registrations ("Nachmeldungen"), e.g. April total from 1,160 MW to 1,250 MW [6].
- A cumulative stock of ground-mounted PV capacity (GW) in operation was not found in any source opened. We compute it from MaStR (`ArtDerSolaranlage` = Freiflächensolaranlage, status In Betrieb). Caveat: BNetzA category definitions are not given in [5]-[7]; likely the MaStR `ArtDerSolaranlage` (see mastr.md), which includes landfill and floating PV, and the category changed on 01.10.2025.

### Rule changes affecting interpretation of requests vs commitments
- Reifegradverfahren: the four ÜNB introduced a maturity-based allocation for storage and large-consumer connection requests, in operation since 01.04.2026 (replacing first-come-first-served); cycles with fixed deadlines, three phases, deposits and construction-cost-subsidy tranches [10] (law-firm and trade-press sources, SECONDARY). A statutory basis (§17a EnWG-E in the Netzanschlusspaket) was a draft before the Bundestag in October 2026 [10]. Consequence: after April 2026 ÜNB "requests" and "commitments" are counted under a different process; a lobby statement mentioned 263 further battery applications in the new procedure [10]. SMARD's 2026 ÜNB figures (23 requests, 64 commitments, to 01.04.2026) straddle the switch [1]. We should not compare 2026 ÜNB counts with 2025 without that caveat.
- KraftNAV: BNetzA states it does not apply to batteries (clarified 23.12.2025); earlier KraftNAV commitments are still to be considered under §17(1) S.1 EnWG; the grid operator may switch procedure until capacity is allocated, if no KraftNAV commitment exists [4]. So some older "commitments" may rest on KraftNAV rather than §17 EnWG.
- Realisierungskaution: BNetzA considers EUR 1,500 per MW connection capacity "nicht unangemessen" [4]. This reduces speculative requests from 2026 and makes later request counts not comparable with 2025.
- BNetzA decision BK6-25-287 (04.05.2026): §17(2a) EnWG means the connection priority of renewable and CHP plants cannot be invoked against storage (search summary, SECONDARY, decision not opened).
- Flexible grid connection agreements (FCA) can reduce connection capacity; so "committed GW" is capacity at the connection point and may differ from nameplate [4].

## What this means for our method
- Use the SMARD article as the source for the "pipeline" framing in the paper, quoting 573.5 GW requested and 54.2 GW / 142.4 GWh committed, with the three caveats above (older requests included, offers not contracts, multi-site duplication). Do not present 54 GW as expected build-out; BNetzA itself calls commitments only "a more realistic indicator".
- Compare our image-derived detections and MaStR with order-of-magnitude context: installed large storage about 2.4 GW gross / 3.2 GWh (Oct 2025, BNetzA) against the 54 GW committed, i.e. the pipeline is more than 20 times the stock; MaStR "in Planung" was about 5.0 GW / 10.4 GWh at the same date [4], which is the registry-visible part of the pipeline and the right comparator for imagery.
- The survey is annual and aggregated, with no unit IDs; it cannot be used for unit-level validation. Only MaStR can.
- Size class: align with BNetzA "> 999 kW gross" for BESS; for solar use ground-mounted in MaStR with Nettonennleistung >= 1 MWp, and report Battery-Charts-style classes (30 kW, 1 MW) only for comparison.
- Cite each reference total with Stichtag, gross/net and basis; sources disagree by 20-40 % depending on class and date.
- Rule changes after April 2026 (Reifegradverfahren, deposits) mean 2026 request and commitment counts will fall or change meaning; use only 2024 and 2025 for trend statements.
