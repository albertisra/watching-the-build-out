## Details

### Typical visible stage sequence (general project practice; no source gives stage dates)

I did not find a primary source that gives stage durations. The sequence below is general knowledge and is UNVERIFIED as a sourced statement; only the project-level durations above are sourced.

BESS:
1. Site clearing and levelling (bare ground; for brownfield sites, demolition or remediation first, e.g. Boxberg cooling towers demolished Dec 2024 [S9]).
2. Access roads and cable trenching.
3. Gravel pad or concrete foundations for containers, inverter/transformer stations (bright, flat, rectangular areas).
4. Delivery and placement of containers in rows: arrives as factory-assembled boxes, so this stage can be quick (Hamm and Neurath: 690 cabinets) and is visible in orthophotos as regular rows.
5. Transformer, switchgear and substation connection; this is the stage that often determines energisation (Boxberg needs new 380 kV GIS [S9]).
6. Commissioning tests (energisation can precede regular operation by weeks to months: Schuby tests since Oct 2026; Alfeld connected Jan 2026, commercial operation planned Aug 2026).
7. Fencing and landscaping as an end stage only.

Solar:
1. Site clearing, possible archaeological or ecological work, boundary works.
2. Access tracks and cable routes.
3. Piling or ramming of mounting posts (regular dot rows; ground is disturbed).
4. Mounting structure installation, then modules (surface changes to dark, regular stripes; bifacial and fixed-tilt arrays).
5. Inverter/transformer stations and own substation, cable to the grid.
6. Fencing as a stage; re-vegetation or grass cover between rows after construction.

Interpretation for time series: Sentinel-1 backscatter changes (metal structures, bare soil) and Sentinel-2 NDVI drops precede visible module arrays; modules are the last visible stage and may be installed section by section, which matches the partial commissioning dates (Weesow, Witznitz). Construction duration from earthworks to modules visible is shorter than start to commissioning if grid connection lags.

### Delays between completion, grid connection and commissioning

- Alttrebbin and Gottesgabe: planned end 2021, first feed-in Feb 2022 (supply shortages, Covid, flooding) [S13].
- Henschleben: physically built second phase (8.5 MW) waiting for a substation expansion, with a Q1 2023 date announced in Nov 2022 [S19].
- Alfeld: grid connection reported Jan 2026, commercial operation planned August (about 6 to 7 months later) [S7].
- Bollingstedt: an earlier trade article is said to have expected grid connection in January 2025; it came in April 2025, about 3 months later (from search summary; the earlier article not opened).
- Wengerohr (Eco Stor, 310.5 MW / 714 MWh): construction began about 1.5 years later than planned; first stage Q4 2027 [S20].
- Waiting-for-connection reporting by Bundesnetzagentur and Tagesspiegel (18,158 connection requests for 573.5 GW of BESS in 2025) appeared in search summaries only; not opened.
- MaStR "Inbetriebnahmedatum" is therefore expected to lag first physical completion by weeks to many months. No study quantifying this lag for Germany was found.

## What this means for our method

1. Detection window: expect imagery-visible activity for about 6 to 20 months (BESS) and 9 to 24 months (solar) before MaStR commissioning; use a 12 to 18 month look-back window as a prior.
2. Registry lag: physical completion may precede MaStR commissioning by months (Alfeld, Henschleben); do not treat "imagery shows complete site, registry empty" as an error before checking grid-connection status.
3. Planned commissioning dates often slip (Alttrebbin about 2 months, Bollingstedt about 3, Wengerohr about 18); do not use announced dates as ground truth.
4. Partial commissioning (Weesow, Witznitz, Foerderstedt stages) means one site can have several MaStR units at different dates.
5. For validation we need MaStR "Inbetriebnahmedatum" for the projects listed; none were checked in this cluster.
